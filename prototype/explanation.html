01 / The problem

Identical copies leave no trail.

A sender encrypts one confidential document. Several authorized recipients decrypt it. If a copy leaks, its identical content does not tell us which issued copy it came from.

The required evidence

Give each decryption session an invisible fingerprint. Have the recipient sign its record with their own post-quantum key. Commit that evidence to a distributed ledger that one administrator cannot rewrite.

02 / Our solution

Record the evidence before releasing the copy.

01

Encrypt once

Protect the document with AES-256-GCM. Give each authorized recipient a separate post-quantum key envelope.

02

Decrypt & fingerprint

A separate managed appliance retains content keys and unmarked originals. Contour marking works experimentally but fails readable shape replacement; a release carrier remains unvalidated.

03

Recipient signs

Recipient-owned keys sign access and exact release approval. The appliance separately signs a processing receipt.

04

Ledger confirms

Verify actual consensus certificates and durable ACKs. The recorded prototype uses four separate Go engines over native PQ TCP, with four ACKs per session and claim. Independent administration remains a deployment requirement.

05

Release exact bytes

Consensus orders a fresh transfer claim against cancellation and revocation. An earlier accepted claim grants one bounded live connection; reconnect needs a new claim.

06

Verify a recovered copy

Verify native signatures and real ledger proofs for exact bytes or complete rendering equality. Changed or unsupported content stays inconclusive.

03 / Implementation

Selected protocol & carrier blocker

Cryptography
OpenSSL 3.5.9 LTS · ML-KEM-768 · ML-DSA-65 · AES-256-GCM
Audit & recovery
SmartBFT 1.0.2 · four networked engine processes · native pure-PQ TLS · opaque PFX signers and encrypted source/prepared/journal stores compose in the test-only prototype

What the evidence means

Signed output and independently verified ledger proofs authenticate a prepared session. Complete pixel equality also supports metadata/resource rewrites. Header transplants and critical-digit edits refuse even when a payload survives.

Association with an issued session does not establish the human leaker. Trusted issuers or appliances can also leak that copy.
04 / Current status
Readable removal reproduced

Contours survive the previous tonal and position removers, but public-font glyph replacement defeats them. All 150 number strings remain correct in each attacked holdout. No validated carrier or perception study.

Working prototype verified

The recorded full baseline passes: 74 protocol checks, 27 independent Go vectors, 12 pure-PQ TCP checks, 73 ledger tests/subtests, 3 direct integrations, 20 PDF verifier checks and 17 actual-chain checks. The working prototype adds 15 integration tests and Go race/vet checks. The visual UI has 13 browser checks. Cohorts overlap; they are not a combined security score.

Current boundary: experimental digital PDFs · separate managed appliance · air-gapped LAN

Recipient owns signing keys; the appliance retains KEM keys. This custody/raster baseline is conditional, not sponsor-approved. One-page ASCII raster tests do not establish native/multilingual support, human invisibility, parser isolation or independent deployment. Native engine and recipient TLS are integrated in the recorded prototype. Protected persistent unlock, restart/status reconciliation, trusted consent UI, hostile parser isolation and installed deployment remain open. Attributed production release stays blocked; photos/scans remain deferred.
